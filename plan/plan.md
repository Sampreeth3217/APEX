# Idea Foundry: AI Tool Finder for Small Businesses

## Platform
Web app

## What it is
A guided tool finder that asks a small-business owner 10 plain questions and recommends up to three AI tools, chosen by a transparent scoring engine.
Each pick comes with its evidence, unknowns, a rough estimate of hours saved and a setup guide. There is also a browsable marketplace, comparisons and a grounded chat helper.

## Who it's for
- **Owners** of salons, restaurants/cafés, plumbing/HVAC, landscaping, cleaning, retail and online stores who aren't technical and have tight budgets.
- **Hackathon judges**, who try three sample businesses without signing in.
- **Tool providers**, who submit listings for review.
- **Admins** (nsc@umd.edu, rishidev@umd.edu, bsrinand@gmail.com), who approve listings, watch AI spending and run better-tool alerts.

## Core features and experience
- **Landing page:** premium, warm and trustworthy. Takes inspiration from G2, Wispr Flow and Grassfeld without copying them. Two buttons: "Find AI for my business" and "Try a sample business". Includes how it works, three example businesses and a preview of a real result.
- **Questionnaire:** the 10 questions from the system design, one per screen. Progress is saved in the browser and survives a refresh, and there's no sign-in until the end.
- **Google sign-in and onboarding:** name, business name, city or ZIP, and an optional phone number. Two separate consent boxes (email and text) start unticked, and their exact wording is saved.
- **Results dashboard:** up to three picks from the scoring engine. Each card shows:
  - why it fits, who it's not for, and what to check first
  - the price or "Not listed"
  - "Works with" software
  - an hours-saved range labelled as a rough estimate
  - an Unknowns box
  - Save, Compare and Set-up buttons

  The dashboard also has a Fit × Trust chart and a "Why these three?" panel. If fewer than three tools qualify, it says why.
- **Sample businesses:** a salon, a restaurant and a plumber at /demo, open to anyone. They're generated by the real scoring engine and clearly labelled as samples.
- **Marketplace and tool pages:** search, category chips, filters for price, free trial and "works with", plus sorting. Each tool page shows its sources, with a flag on sources written by the company or a competitor. It also shows which details are estimated, its unknowns, warnings about tools built for bigger businesses, the last-checked date, and public comments that show first names only.
- **Compare:** two or three tools side by side, with the differences highlighted and a tradeoff summary grounded in the records.
- **Setup guides:** each guide has the six required headings and is written for the owner's tech comfort. It includes the AI disclaimer, saves which steps are checked off and has helpful/not-helpful buttons.
- **Chat helper:** on every page. It only uses catalog data through the four allowed functions. If the AI is off, it shows a graceful fallback.
- **My tools:** saved tools marked considering, using or dropped, plus comments, notifications and settings for phone, location and consent.
- **Providers and admin:** providers submit listings that stay pending. Admins approve them and see AI spending, cache hits, fallbacks and the budget guard. A clearly labelled demo action adds a better tool and runs the alerts.

## Assumptions
- The stack is the one named in the master prompt, not Emergent's default stack, and the code stays a portable Next.js project.
- Data and sign-in live in the user's own Supabase project. Its URL and keys come during the build, and Google sign-in is set up in the Supabase dashboard.
- AI runs through the user's OpenRouter key and the two models the user picks (a cheap one and a mid-tier one). These come during the build, and the app works with AI switched off until then.
- Email alerts use an email provider of the user's choice. Its name and key come during the build, and until then alerts are in-app only.
- The admin list is nsc@umd.edu, rishidev@umd.edu and bsrinand@gmail.com, checked on the server.
- There are no sponsored rankings, billing or ads.
- Footer details (Privacy, Terms, Contact) are clearly marked placeholders until the user provides real ones.
- Catalog dates stay as given in the source (2026-11-07) and are not re-verified.
